# Claims and difficult questions for the SCIDIP panel

This is a technical evidence guide, not the final pitch deck or a patent opinion. Submitted Office documents remain unchanged and must be reconciled in later phases.

| Question or historical claim | Defensible answer after Phase 1 |
|---|---|
| What have you built? | A reproducible composition-and-gradient inverse-screening pipeline, with protected-group model evaluation, reduced thermal mechanics, measurement ingestion and documented evidence gaps. |
| Is it a closed-loop discovery demonstration? | The software supports proposal, report-backed ingestion and retraining. No new physical measurement cycle was completed; an experimentally validated loop is the next milestone. |
| What are the AI models? | Fixed ExtraTrees regressors for static calculated bulk modulus and experimental compression yield; yield also uses temperature and processing category. Thermal properties and phase descriptors are explicit estimates, not hidden AI models. |
| Is R² about 0.98 proof of new-alloy performance? | No. The historical split overlapped formula groups, and the historical yield score was a training score. New protected-composition scores are 0.867 bulk and 0.457 compression yield on small held-out groups. |
| Have you proven a single-phase BCC alloy? | No. VEC, entropy and size mismatch are heuristics. Composition-specific assessed CALPHAD and manufactured phase characterization are missing. |
| Has CALPHAD run? | The optional adapter was exercised on a real Al–Ni equilibrium fixture. It has not validated the nine-element target because an applicable assessed database was not supplied. |
| Do you have a safety factor above 1.5? | No qualified safety factor is established. The old ratio mixed incomparable properties/stress meanings. The current output is peak in-plane elastic laminate stress with explicit assumptions. |
| Can it operate at 1400 °C? | That has not been demonstrated. Historical code used 1400 K (1126.85 °C). The new comparison labels 1400 K and 1673.15 K (1400 °C) separately as imposed face temperatures. |
| Does a small Al addition guarantee self-healing oxidation protection? | No. The old post-optimization addition invalidated composition/model consistency. Phase 1 excludes unmodeled Al and requires actual scale/oxidation evidence. |
| Is the new top alloy better? | It is a computational hypothesis under a declared score. Broad yield bounds, extrapolated bulk predictions and missing phase/oxidation/manufacture evidence prevent a superiority claim. |
| Is the 11-layer recipe production-ready? | It is a mass/volume-consistent coupon design with exact geometry and full chemistry. Process, shrinkage, porosity, feedstock and bond qualification remain open. |
| Is this turbine FEA, DFT or a lifetime simulation? | No. The executed model is steady conduction plus a reduced elastic laminate. No new target-alloy DFT, 3-D component FEA, MD, creep integration or fatigue-life simulation was performed. |
| Why Sona Comstar? | Precision forging is an evidenced activity, making tooling-coupon durability a specific discovery conversation. The proposed pilot and its temperatures are hypotheses awaiting customer requirements. |
| What is the moat or patent? | Not established in Phase 1. Candidate novelty, claim scope, disclosure timeline, data rights and freedom to operate require Phase 2 assessment. |
| What is the strongest current evidence? | Executable code, 30 verification/integration checks, full source-row and run provenance, fair-budget search comparison, exact candidate vectors and honest negative uncertainty results. |
| What would you do with support? | Resolve one customer's requirements, obtain applicable thermodynamic/manufacturing support, make controlled coupons, measure phase/oxidation/bond/thermal properties, and demonstrate one prospective feedback iteration against a baseline. |

Avoid claiming certified aerospace readiness, experimental self-healing, guaranteed grain/phase stability, 10,000-cycle durability, measured startup ROI, a Sona partnership or a patentable invention from the present computational record.
