# Primary sources consulted

Sources were checked during the Phase 1 work beginning 20 October 2026. These references support the methodological choices and application context; they do not validate the project's unmeasured alloys. External content is summarized, not reproduced.

1. **FITT / IIT Delhi, SCIDIP program.** https://fitt-iitd.in/web/sona-comstar-iit-delhi-innovation-program-scidip — supports evaluation against technical strength, entrepreneurial intent, IP, team and business plan, and the need for translational proof-of-concept evidence. Verify applicable terms with FITT; this report does not assert grant approval or change the submitted proposal.
2. **Sona Comstar, Precision Forged Gears.** https://sonacomstar.com/precision-forged-bevel-gears — the company describes warm/hot precision-forged driveline products. A tooling research pilot is our inference; no internal company process data or partnership is claimed.
3. **Li et al., A critical examination of robustness and generalizability of machine learning prediction of materials properties, npj Computational Materials (2023), DOI 10.1038/s41524-023-01012-9.** Author/NIST copy: https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=935648 — materials benchmark success may not transfer under distribution shift; motivates explicit applicability and external validation rather than a single favorable split.
4. **Borg et al., Expanded dataset of mechanical properties and observed phases of multi-principal element alloys, Scientific Data (2020), DOI 10.1038/s41597-020-00768-9.** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-020-00768-9 ; authors' source repository: https://github.com/CitrineInformatics/MPEA_dataset — provenance for the public MPEA collection. Local source-file hashes and row/DOI links identify what the model actually used, rather than counting every database entry as a training observation.
5. **pycalphad official documentation and source.** https://pycalphad.org/docs/latest/examples/EquilibriumWithOrdering.html and https://github.com/pycalphad/pycalphad — database-dependent equilibrium calculations, independent component constraints, vacancies and entered phases. Runtime used pycalphad 0.11.2. The Al–Ni test database is consumed from its installed package; no commercial TDB is redistributed. Library reference: Otis & Liu (2017), DOI 10.5334/jors.140.
6. **Forming protective alumina scale for ductile refractory high-entropy alloys via aluminizing, Intermetallics 123 (2020), 106838.** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intermet.2020.106838 — protective alumina was tied to specific alloy/process treatment. It does not justify asserting that an arbitrary small Al addition provides oxidation protection for a different optimized chemistry.
7. **Zhu & Miller, Thermal Barrier Coatings for Advanced Gas Turbine and Diesel Engines, NASA/TM-1999-209453.** https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/20000003013.pdf — coating durability involves cyclic thermomechanical conditions, ceramic evolution and bond-coat oxidation; a steady thermal stress estimate alone is incomplete.
8. **NASA, Thermal barrier coating life prediction model development, document 19890004250.** https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19890004250 — bond-coat oxidation, creep and spallation motivate separate durability evidence and prevent equating an elastic stress screen with life prediction.

No numerical target-alloy validation result has been borrowed from these papers. Elemental mixture constants remain inherited reference estimates requiring measurement; their use is stated explicitly in the code and workflow rather than being passed off as literature-calibrated alloy behavior.
